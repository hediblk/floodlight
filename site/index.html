The model

How the forecasts are made

Rate every team, price every match, then play the season out 10,000 times. Every percentage is a share of those runs.

10K
Three steps

  1. 01Rate the teams

    Attack and defence from goals and expected goals, recent games counting more. An Elo rating tracks results alone.

  2. 02Price every match

    Home, draw and away chances from both models, blended, with likely scorelines from the goal model.

  3. 03Play out the season

    Every fixture left is simulated and the table ranked with the competition's own tie-breakers.

Reading the numbers
Ranges
The middle 80% of simulated seasons: match-to-match luck, not doubt about the ratings.
Zones
Finishing places. Cup winners can change real European spots; deciding play-offs aren't simulated.
Calls
Each played match is forecast at 00:00 UTC on match day, from earlier results only. A call is right when the likeliest outcome happens.
Champions League
One model
Every entrant's domestic and European results feed one goal model, with each league on a common scale. Bookmaker prices for upcoming games keep league levels current.
League phase
All 144 fixtures are simulated and ranked with UEFA's tie-breakers.
Knockouts
Simulated draws follow the bracket and seeding rules; shoot-outs are a coin toss. Title chances average every draw.
Club ratings
Rating
The share of points a club would take off an average European top-flight side on a neutral pitch, from the Champions League model.
Attack, defence
Attack rates a club as if its defence matched its attack, and defence the reverse, so all three share one scale. Scores and Concedes are goals per game against the average side.
Across leagues
Leagues are linked by European cup games. The average side is the mean of every club with domestic results in the past year. League averages: loading.
What we compare against

A forecast's score means little on its own, so every score on this site sits beside simpler forecasts made for the same games.

Blind guess
One in three for home, draw and away in every game. It knows nothing, and scores a log loss of 1.099. Any useful forecast must beat it.
Prior frequency
How often home wins, draws and away wins happened in the competition over the five seasons before this one, given unchanged to every game (each competition's shares are listed under Track record). It knows home advantage but nothing about either team, so beating it is the first real test. "vs baseline" on Matches is this comparison.
Elo
The results-only rating inside our own blend (the 16% in the blend above), scored alone. Beating it shows what goals, expected goals and the goal model add. It is compared only on games where its own chances were saved with the forecast.
Bookmakers
Odds are archived every day but not yet compared: too few games have prices saved before kickoff. (Champions League strengths already use them, so that record is not market-free.)
Reading the track record
Published
Forecasts the site actually showed before kickoff, saved with a timestamp. These are the receipts.
Reconstructed
Games played before the site saved receipts, forecast afterwards with the same model from results available before 00:00 UTC on match day. Nothing from the game itself leaks in, but they were never shown in advance.
Combined
Every finished game counted once: its published forecast where there is one, otherwise the reconstructed one. This is the default.
Log loss
The main score: the average of −ln(chance given to what happened). Giving the actual result 50% costs 0.69, 33% costs 1.10 and 10% costs 2.30, so confident misses hurt most. Lower is better.
Brier, RPS
Two more error scores, lower is better. Brier adds up the squared errors on home, draw and away. RPS (ranked probability score) treats the outcomes as ordered, so calling a home win is closer to a draw than to an away win.
Called
How often the single likeliest outcome happened. Easy to read, but it ignores how confident the forecast was.
Exact score
How often the single likeliest scoreline happened.
Avg chance given
The average chance the forecast gave to the outcome that actually happened.
Track record

Baselines and coverage

Prior frequency uses the five seasons before this one. Elo compares only fixtures with a saved Elo forecast from the same run. Gap = model minus benchmark on the games both forecast; negative is better.

Published: saved before kickoff. Reconstructed: rebuilt from results before match day; each fixture counts once. The seasons used for calibration aren't independent holdouts, and Champions League strengths already use market prices.

Limits
Fixed ratings
Transfers, injuries and new managers count only once they show in results.
Calibration
Weights were tuned on single matches, not final tables.
Data gaps
Postponed games are played as if rescheduled. Clubs outside the five leagues have no xG, so they're rated on goals.
